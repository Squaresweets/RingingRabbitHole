Hi there, my name is Matthew and welcome to the fourth episode of the ringing rabbit hole. In the last episode we covered place notation, but today I want to discuss coursing order. This is a reasonably complicated topic so if you are unsure about any topics I've discussed so far I'd reccomend you revise them.

I'd like to start this video by introducing you to this sequence of numbers. It is the odd numbered bells decreasing and then the even numbered bells increasing. In this example I am assuming we have 8 bells. This sequence and most other sequences I show in this video should be treated as cyclic, and thus can be rotated like this while staying identical. For example, these two sequences are identical as one can be rotated to get the other.

Let's now see where we can find this sequence in plain hunt major. First is the order the bells lead in. This makes sense as even bells hunt in and odd bells hunt out at the start, so it makes sense we will get one after the other. If we look at all of the bells the treble rings after you can see the same pattern, excluding the treble as it can't follow itself. This is the same for all bells in this lead as after leading the bells will hunt out, eventualy crossing with paths with another bell.

Now let's look at plain bob major, and bring back the circle of work from episode 1. The numbers on the outside represent the different possible place bells and the numbers on the inside represent which bell is doing each place bell's work. For example here the 5 is doing the work 3 did in the first lead at this lead. Unraveling this inner sequence in any orientation gives us our sequence, minus the treble. If we now go to the next lead and unravel it again we still get our sequence, as the rotation maintains the order of the sequence.

Let's focus on the bells which the bell in 8ths place follows at each lead. In the first lead it follows these bells, giving us our sequence. In the next lead we can use our circle of work to work out what bells we follow. Where we saw the 3 in the first lead we instead see the bell now in 3rds place, the 5. Essentially all of the bells in the sequence have been rotated around but the treble has stayed in the same place. This gives rise to two ways of thinking of coursing order, either in terms of the treble or in terms of minimal changes between leads. If you think of it in terms of the treble you rotate the coursing order in your head at each lead to keep the treble at the start. This tells you exactly where the treble will be when hunting. However since the treble never changes it's path it is more efficient just to ignore it. Since the rest of the bells just rotate and a sequence is cyclic we can just remember the same sequence between leads.

Let's look at what happens when we have a call. Since the circle of work flattened is the same as the coursing order I am going to use it for this visulisation, however we need to decide where we are unwrapping from. Even though any unwrapping is valid we want to use the one which is simplest to remember. The first option is to unwrap around a fixed point which doesn't move when rotating. We can choose where this fixed point is but typically it is where the treble would be so that we can write out our coursing order like this and keep track of where the treble is. Once again this isn't that useful for this method but it will come in handy later. This is our coursing order before and after a plain lead, giving us this transposition. This is it before and after a bob lead. And after a single lead. Essentially all you are doing is keeping track of a transposition of the current lead head, with this specific transposition being easy to remember and useful in keeping track of where people should be.
The alternative is to have our unwrapping point move with our rotations. This has the advantage of us not having to mentally rotate the bells at every lead but has the disadvantage of not telling us which bells are effected by calls. To do that you need to keep track of what calling position we are at (which place bell the tenor is) and use that to work out which bells are here and so which part of the coursing order needs to have this transposition applied.

To use coursing order while ringing you have to keep this sequence in your head and make differnet transpositions at each call depending on the calling position. But once you get used to doing that in your head you can predict every bell which you need to follow and when every bell is leading. In plain bob it is very easy to see the coursing order, but for other methods it can be more complicated.
