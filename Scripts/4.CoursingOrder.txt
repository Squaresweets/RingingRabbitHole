The numbers on the outside represent the different place bells and the numbers on the inside represent which bell is doing each place bell's work. For example here the 5 is doing the work 3 did in the first lead at this lead. Unraveling this inner sequence in any orientation gives us our sequence, minus the treble. If we now go to the next lead and unravel it again we still get our sequence, as the rotation maintains the order of the sequence.

Let's focus on the bells which the bell in 8ths place follows at each lead. In the first lead it follows these bells, giving us our sequence. In the next lead we can use our circle of work to work out what bells we follow. Where we saw the 3 in the first lead we instead see the bell now in 3rds place, the 5. Essentially all of the bells in the sequence have been rotated around but the treble has stayed in the same place. We want to keep track of this sequence between leads, and the treble staying in the same place but the rest of the bells rotating gives rise to two ways of thinking of coursing order, either in terms of the treble or in terms of minimal changes between leads. If you think of it in terms of the treble you rotate the coursing order in your head at each lead to keep the treble at the start. This tells you exactly where the treble will be when hunting. However since the treble never changes its path it is more efficient just to ignore it. Since the rest of the bells just rotate and a sequence is cyclic we can just remember the same sequence between leads. The first way of unwrapping is to unwrap around a fixed point which doesn't move when rotating. We can choose where this fixed point is but typically it is where the treble would be so that we can write out our coursing order like this and keep track of where the treble is. The alternative is to have our unwrapping point move with our rotations. This has the advantage of us not having to mentally rotate the bells at every lead but has the disadvantage of not telling us which bells are effected by calls. We know that the bells in these three positions will be effected, but since we haven't kept track of the number of rotations between calls we don't know which bells are there. To get around this you need to keep track of what calling position we are at (which place bell the tenor is) when the call is made and use that to work out which bells are here and so which part of the coursing order needs to have this transposition applied. For example, if we have a call at the first lead or when the tenor is in at wrong (7nths place) the bells that are effected are going to be the 3 bells in this corner, and so unwraveling we get this transposition if we get a bob at wrong. We can repeat this process for all calling positions and for singles. Note that by using the calling positions all we are doing is keeping track of which number rotation we are on, and many compositions just use a number for this. Naming the leads with calling positions makes it easier to work out as you go along, but if the observation bell is effected this can mess up the order and so you may need to resort to numbering leads. The transpositions also tell us what each bell is doing when the call is made as the place bells of the 3 effected are consistent. This method is equivalent to keeping a transposition of the current course head in your head, assuming the observation bell is uneffected.

A lot of compositions are known as tenors together, this means that any bell greater than 6 is not effected by calls, so this section of the coursing order never changes, and so is often ignored. If the tenors aren't kept together it is known as split tenors. 

To use coursing order while ringing you have to keep this sequence in your head and make differnet transpositions at each call depending on the calling position. But once you get used to doing that in your head you can predict every bell which you need to follow and when every bell is leading. When we say that we are coursing a bell it means that we are after it in the coursing order, which means that if a bell sees that bell they are likely to see us after.
